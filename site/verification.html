DA test plan

Thank you for testing DA. This plan walks through everything DA does, step by step: first with the menu (what most people use), then with commands typed in a terminal (for things the menu doesn't have). Each step says what to do and what you should see. Mark it passed when it works, skipped when you can't try it, and failed when it doesn't: write down the step's number (like D7) and what happened, and see Reporting a problem.

On the website this page keeps track for you: fill in your name at the top (and the version, which is filled in already), then mark each step with its buttons: ✓ passed (green), ○ skipped (yellow) or ✗ failed (red); press it again to clear it. The page counts your progress and remembers it - in this browser, on this computer only. Each step's Report button opens a new problem report with the step, your name and the version already filled in, and marks the step failed. Problems already reported for a step (by you or anyone) are listed under it, with whether they're still open and their labels. Save as PDF keeps a dated copy of your results; when a new version of DA comes out, Start again on a new version clears them for the next round. On paper, use the printable checklist.

The website has one Start timing button. When you're ready, start it; the first unfinished step is timed. Choose that step's passed, skipped or failed result to save its time and automatically move timing to the next unfinished step. Use Pause timing for breaks; resume when you're ready. Each section's time is the sum of its steps, and the overall time is the sum of all section times. Timing is saved in this browser along with your results; starting again on a new version clears it.

You don't need to do it all in one sitting: each part starts from where the one before left off. The whole plan takes a few evenings. The user guide explains anything this plan mentions; the known problems list what's already known, so you don't need to report those.

Before you start

  • Use copies, never your only photos. DA never deletes anything and asks before it moves anything, but this is a test: copy some photos into a new folder and test with those.
  • A Windows PC with about 5 GB free, and an internet connection for a few steps (places, updates).
  • Write down your DA version (it's shown in step A5) when you report anything.
  • Words used here: an archive is the folder DA looks after; intake is where you put new files; a sidecar is the small .meta.json file DA keeps beside each photo; the menu is what opens when you double-click an archive's Open DA.cmd (or type da).

Make a test kit

Make a folder C:\DA-test-kit and put copies of these in it. The more kinds you have, the more of the plan you can try; skip a step whose files you don't have and mark it skipped.

Folder in the kit What to put in it Used in
phone 20-50 photos from a phone (JPG or HEIC), some taken outdoors (they have a location) C, H, K, L
phone 2-3 iPhone Live Photos (each is a .HEIC or .JPG plus a .MOV with the same name) I
phone 2-3 ordinary videos (.mov or .mp4, longer than 5 seconds) I
camera 5-10 photos from a different camera (another phone, or a digital camera) H, K
scans 5 scans of old prints (JPG/PNG/TIFF that have no date in them or in their name) G, J
docs 3-5 PDFs (bills, letters) and a Word or Excel file G
doubles 3 photos that are exact copies of photos in phone (copy them again) F
old 3-5 photos taken in a different decade from the rest (say 2005 if the rest are 2020s) M
other an old camera video (.AVI / .MPG), a .CR2/.NEF raw file with its JPG, a screenshot D, I

Reporting a problem

  1. Check the known problems first.
  2. On the website, press the step's Report button: a new issue opens at GitHub with the step, your name and your version filled in (a free GitHub account is needed). Or open one yourself at https://github.com/devmukmuk/DA/issues, or send it to the person who asked you to test. The panel at the top shows how many of your reports are still open.
  3. Include: the step number (L9), what you did, what you expected, what happened (a screenshot helps), your DA version (da version), and whether DA is the DA.exe or the Python install.
  4. If DA printed an error, the archive's logs\da.jsonl has the details: copy the last few lines.
  5. Leave out anything private: names, photos, and folder paths you'd rather not share.
